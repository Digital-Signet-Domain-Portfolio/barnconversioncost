Surveyor's note

Build-cost bands are the site's own indicative model, inflated to a Q2 2026 footing via the ONS Construction Output Price Index (April 2026). Class Q rules current to the 21 May 2024 GPDO amendment.

This page sets out where the numbers come from and what the site does not pretend to know. The buyer is paying £350k-£800k on a building decision; transparency on method is the trust currency, not a glossy methodology page.

Where the £/m² bands come from

The bands are our own indicative scoping model, not figures lifted from a single published dataset. We start from a base build-cost per m² by spec tier (below) and adjust it by barn type, condition, planning route and region. The base rates reflect typical UK agricultural-to-residential conversion build costs; we inflate them to a Q2 2026 footing using ONS Construction Output Price Indices (April 2026 release). For an authoritative benchmark on a specific building, a chartered quantity surveyor can price it against RICS BCIS (a subscription cost database); we do not reproduce a specific BCIS release here.

The three spec tiers

  • Modest, £1,800/m² base: standard kitchen, vinyl flooring, basic sanitaryware, simple finishes.
  • Mid, £2,500/m² base: branded kitchen, engineered oak flooring, mid-tier sanitaryware, UFH throughout, integrated joinery.
  • High, £3,400/m² base: bespoke kitchen, stone or natural-fibre floor, high-end sanitaryware, joinery throughout, exposed services as feature.

The multipliers

Barn-type, condition, planning-route and region multipliers are documented in src/data/calc-multipliers.ts. They are the model's own judgement factors, calibrated to typical build-cost differentials rather than a published index. Total multiplier = base × barn-type × condition × planning × region.

What the calculator does not do

  • It does not give a fixed quote. It gives a band. Treat low-mid-high as the model's cautious, central and stretched case, not a statistical percentile from a measured dataset.
  • It does not include land purchase, demolition, or any commercial-to-residential conversion (use VAT Notice 708 advice for those).
  • It does not include equestrian or livery conversions; the Class Q regime does not cover them.
  • It does not predict outcome on borderline Class Q applications; refusal-risk premium is a separate read.
